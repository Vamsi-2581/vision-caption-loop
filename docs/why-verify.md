# Why verify before you trust a caption

A caption is a **claim** about what is in the frame. Models (and people) love fluent claims.

## The trap

Draft caption:

> A shop photo shows a rusty rotor near a brake, pad, shop part.

It *sounds* right. It even names brake and pad. It never says **rotor** as a verified tag — wait, it says rusty rotor in the cue half, but the teaching check is: did every **must-tag** appear as a named part you would write on a work order?

If the work order needs `brake`, `pad`, and `rotor`, a caption that waves at “shop part” fails the job.

## The loop

1. **Caption** — say what you see.
2. **Verify** — score against must-tags (parts that must be named).
3. **Refine** — only when something is missing; don’t rewrite for style alone.

Shop rule: name the part before the story. “Spongy pedal” is a symptom. “Worn pad on a rusty rotor” is a caption a tech can act on.
